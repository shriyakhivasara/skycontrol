# Glossary

Aviation words used in SkyControl, in plain English.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Approach** | The last part of a flight, flying towards the airport to land. In SkyControl a flight appears on radar 30 minutes out. |
| **Holding** | Circling near the airport because the runway is not free yet. Burns fuel. |
| **Wake turbulence** | Swirling air left behind an aircraft's wings. Big aircraft leave strong wake, so the next aircraft must wait longer. |
| **Separation** | The minimum gap between two aircraft using the same runway. In SkyControl: 1 to 3 minutes depending on the wake categories of the leader and the follower. |
| **Wake category** | Light, medium or heavy, based on aircraft size. An A380 is heavy, an A320 is medium, a G650 business jet is light. |
| **Narrowbody / Widebody** | Single-aisle aircraft (A320, B737) / twin-aisle aircraft (A380, B777). |
| **Turnaround** | Time on the ground between arriving and leaving again: unloading, cleaning, refuelling, boarding. |
| **Gate** | A parking spot at the terminal with a jet bridge. |
| **Remote stand** | A parking spot away from the terminal. Passengers go by bus, so turnaround takes longer. |
| **Pushback** | Leaving the gate to start taxiing for take-off. |
| **MAYDAY** | The international distress call for an emergency (engine failure, medical emergency, fire). The flight gets top priority. |
| **Low fuel** | A flight running short of fuel tells the tower, which should let it land sooner. |
| **Fuel reserve** | The fuel a flight must keep to reach another airport safely. When a circling flight gets down to it, it must divert. |
| **Diversion** | Landing at a different airport (Abu Dhabi in SkyControl) instead of the planned one. |
| **Runway 12L / 12R** | Runways are named by their compass direction divided by 10 (120° → 12), with L and R for left and right when there are two parallel ones. |
| **FCFS** | First come, first served: aircraft use the runway in the order they asked. |
| **Movements** | Landings plus take-offs. |
| **On time** | Within 15 minutes of the scheduled time (the standard used by airlines). |
| **Hub bank / wave** | A burst of arrivals close together, then a burst of departures, so passengers can connect. Emirates runs its Dubai hub this way; the schedule copies the idea with three waves. |
